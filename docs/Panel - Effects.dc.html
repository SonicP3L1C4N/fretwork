Effects panel — board and bench

The chain is always visible as a strip of tiles in signal order, from the instrument to the pair of ports; the knobs belong to whichever tile is selected and are drawn once, beneath, at the width of the window. What is folded away is only the knobs of the plugins nobody is turning — the chain itself never is, because what a part goes through on its way out is the question the panel exists to answer. Reordering moves onto the cable: the handle between two tiles swaps them, which is moveEffect(n, +1) and nothing else. Click a tile to move the bench.

Build notes, as shipped. The bench fits the plugin standing on it and stops at gridUnit * 8.8, the height of two rows of 46px knobs: a cabinet's three knobs cost the score three knobs, and a plugin with more than two rows scrolls inside the bench rather than taking the music with it. A ceiling rather than a fixed height — fixing it holds the band still at the price of a hand's depth of empty ink under every small plugin. Measured on this machine with an amplifier, a cabinet and a reverb: the band is 365px with the amplifier on the bench and 280px with the cabinet, against 390px before, when the same chain also cost a scrollbar in each direction and left the reverb off the side of the window. Full width is what buys it: the tape, five-across knobs and the switches sit side by side in one 160px band instead of the 234px stack a card as wide as the mixer forces. The board scrolls sideways when the chain outruns it; the bench only ever scrolls down, and only past the cap. Voicings sits in the bench header rather than under the cassette, because a stage with no voicing yet has no cassette to sit beside.
